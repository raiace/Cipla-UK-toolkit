Section 03 · Work Culture

The Spanish Workplace

Relationship-driven, hierarchy-aware, lunch-serious, and punctuality-flexible. Adjust your expectations before your first meeting.

01 · Core Principle
The long lunch is a business meeting
Business lunches in Spain last 1.5–2.5 hours and involve wine, multiple courses, and serious conversation. Eating quickly and returning to your desk signals you are not investing in relationships. If a Spanish colleague invites you to lunch — stay for the full meal.
02 · Core Principle
Hierarchy exists — relationships cut across it
Spanish organisations have clear hierarchy. But if you have built genuine trust with someone, they will help you regardless of their position. Invest in the relationship first, and professional benefits follow naturally.
03 · Core Principle
Time is flexible — professionally and socially
Business meetings may start 5–10 minutes late. Social events: 20–30 minutes late. Dinner at 9pm means 9:30pm. This is cultural rhythm. Arriving exactly on time for a Spanish dinner can make you the first guest by 30 minutes.

🇮🇳 Key differences for Indian professionals

  • More relaxed formality — first-name basis in most Spanish companies
  • The long lunch 2pm–4pm is non-negotiable — block it off in your calendar
  • Debate and push-back in meetings is healthy, not disrespectful
  • Siesta: Cultural concept, not modern business practice — offices open all day
  • August: No critical decisions — Spain is on vacation Aug 1–20

💬 Communication & Tools

  • WhatsApp: Used for informal follow-up; email is primary for formal communication
  • Meetings: Lively and animated — talking over each other signals interest
  • Regional sensitivity: In Barcelona, acknowledge Catalan culture warmly
  • Networking: The people who get things done in Spain are the ones who know everyone

⭐ The menú del día is your best friend. €10–18 for 3 courses, a drink, and bread. Available weekdays at almost every restaurant. It is how Spaniards eat lunch every day.

☕ Coffee & wine rules

  • Café con leche = morning only
  • After noon: café solo (espresso) or cortado (small white)
  • Milky coffee after 12pm immediately marks you as a tourist
  • Wine at lunch is normal — one or two glasses is the social standard
  • ¡Salud! is the toast — learn it before your first dinner

  • Spain is meat and seafood heavy — say 'soy vegetariano/a'
  • Vegan growing in Malasaña (Madrid) and Gràcia (Barcelona)
  • Indian food: Lavapiés (Madrid), El Raval (Barcelona)
  • Spanish cuisine is NOT spicy by Indian standards
  • Pork is everywhere — always confirm dietary restrictions with staff

Section 06 · Daily Living

Daily Life in Spain

The small things nobody briefed you on — until now. Including the Sunday shutdown.

🛒 Groceries

  • Mercadona — best value, excellent quality, everywhere in Spain
  • Carrefour (large hypermarkets), Lidl & Aldi (budget, great quality)
  • El Corte Inglés Supermercado — premium, open Sundays
  • Mercado de San Miguel (Madrid), La Boqueria (Barcelona) — fresh produce, social ritual
  • Indian groceries: Lavapiés (Madrid), El Raval (Barcelona)
  • Online: Mercadona, Carrefour, El Corte Inglés — set up Week 1

📱 SIM & Banking

  • SIM: Buy Day 1 at Orange, Vodafone or Movistar — bring passport. Plans from €15/month.
  • Best for short stays: Orange or Vodafone. eSIM available if supported.
  • Banking: N26 or Revolut — open online before travel, no fees, works immediately
  • Longer stays: CaixaBank or BBVA with passport on arrival
  • Bizum: Set up via Spanish bank app if staying 1 month+

🧹 Domestic Help & Laundry

  • Domestic help ('asistenta'): €12–18/hour via agencies or TaskRabbit Spain
  • Less common than Brazil or India — more self-service culture here
  • Serviced apartments: Weekly cleaning usually included
  • Laundromats (lavanderías): Available in all major cities

📅 Sunday in Spain

  • Sunday = family and rest. Most small shops and restaurants close.
  • El Corte Inglés and some Carrefour remain open — plan groceries Saturday
  • Business calls on Sunday are not expected
  • Museums busiest on Sundays — go early or on a weekday instead

Section 07 · Housing & Healthcare

Where to Live & the Green Cross

The farmacia (green cross) solves most non-emergency issues instantly. Private insurance handles everything else. Know both before you land.

🏨 Best option: Serviced apartment

  • Fully furnished, bills included, flexible term. €1,800–3,500/month 1BR.
  • Madrid: Salamanca, Chamberí, Retiro, Justicia — safe, central, well-connected
  • Barcelona: Eixample, Sarrià-Sant Gervasi, Gràcia — safe, expat-friendly

🏩 Hotels

  • Hyatt, Marriott, NH Hotels, and Meliá (Spain's premium domestic chain)
  • Airbnb well-established — check reviews carefully
  • Safest: Eixample Barcelona, Salamanca Madrid
  • Costs usually arranged by Cipla HR for short stays

🏠 Private Rental

  • Competitive market — quality apartments go fast in both cities
  • NIE required — apply at Comisaría or with HR's support 2–3 weeks before arrival
  • Deposit: Typically 2 months rent + first month upfront
  • Furnished apartments often offer flexible 3–6 month contracts

💡 HR Global Mobility

  • Can assist with NIE registration — contact 2–3 weeks before arrival
  • Lease support and landlord negotiation
  • Visa, tax, and banking setup support
  • Contact before starting your own housing search

Healthcare in Spain

🏥 Private — Seguro Médico (use this)

  • Cipla provides private insurance — confirm network (Sanitas, Adeslas, or Mapfre) with HR before travel
  • Hospital Clínica Quirónsalud: Best private network, English-speaking, most recommended
  • Hospital La Paz (Madrid): Major hospital, international patients unit
  • Hospital Valle Hebrón (Barcelona): Excellent emergency care
  • Always carry your insurance card — without it you pay upfront
  • Use Sanitas/Adeslas app to book same-day appointments

🏛️ Public & Farmacias

  • Spain has excellent public healthcare — one of the best in Europe
  • Urgencias (A&E): Any hospital must treat you for emergencies regardless of insurance
  • Farmacia (green cross): Pharmacists advise and prescribe some medications — brilliant for minor issues
  • Farmacia de guardia: 24-hour — closed farmacias list nearest open one on their door
  • Indian medicines: Bring 3-month supply — Spanish brand names differ
  • Emergency: Call 112 for all emergencies — works anywhere in the EU

Section 08 · Customs & Language

Social Customs & Spanish Essentials

You don't need to be fluent. Even 'hola, gracias, la cuenta, ¡Salud!' earns immediate warmth. Spanish is phonetic — what you see is what you say.

01 · Norm
Dos besos — two cheek kisses
The standard Spanish greeting among people who know each other — two cheek kisses, left cheek first. In business, handshakes on first meeting. Over time, as trust builds, physical greetings become warmer. Lean in and engage warmly — pulling back reads as coldness.
02 · Norm
Spain is loud — and that's engagement
Spanish social settings are genuinely loud. Everyone talking at once, interrupting, laughing across the table — this is engagement, not chaos. At work, meetings can be lively and animated. People talking over each other during debate shows they are interested and engaged.
03 · Norm
Regionalism is real and matters deeply
A Catalan in Barcelona, a Basque in Bilbao, and an Andalusian in Seville have genuinely different cultures. Acknowledge regional identity — never dismiss it or confuse it with being 'just Spanish'. Asking about their city or regional food is always a great conversation starter.

Three Scenarios — What Would You Do?

Spain rewards warmth, patience with timing, and staying for dessert. Click your answer.

1

The 2:30pm Meeting

You schedule an important call with two Spanish colleagues for 2:30pm on a Tuesday. One joins 20 minutes late. The other sends a message: 'still at lunch, can we push to 4pm?'
✅ Answer: C is correct

2pm–4pm is prime lunch time in Spain — this is your calendar error, not bad behaviour. The correct move is to reschedule without comment and learn the golden rule: never book over the Spanish lunch window. Option A is culturally tone-deaf. Option B creates a problem where there isn't one.

2

The Business Lunch

A Spanish client invites you to a business lunch. It is 3:15pm, the main course just arrived, and you have a meeting back at the office at 4pm. What do you do?
✅ Answer: B is correct

The Spanish business lunch is a relationship event — leaving early signals the relationship matters less than your next calendar slot. Move the meeting. The client relationship has more long-term value than a 4pm internal call. Option C partially works but still signals disengagement.

3

The Barcelona Colleague

You are introduced to a new colleague based in Barcelona. She mentions her pride in being Catalan and that she finds it frustrating when people dismiss Catalonia as 'just another Spanish region'.
✅ Answer: B is correct

Catalan identity is deeply meaningful — dismissing it (Option A) is a significant relationship error. Genuine curiosity about Barcelona and Catalan culture builds trust immediately and is always the right response. Option C is neutral but misses a valuable opportunity to connect warmly.

Safety in Spain

✅ Spain is generally very safe

  • Violent crime against visitors is rare — main risk is pickpocketing
  • Night safety: Cities are safe after dark — outdoor social life continues late
  • Normal urban caution applies — stay in busy areas after midnight
  • Use Cabify after midnight in unfamiliar areas

⚠️ Pickpocket hotspots

  • Las Ramblas (Barcelona), Sagrada Família queue, La Boqueria
  • Puerta del Sol and Gran Vía (Madrid), Metro Line 1 tourist routes
  • Prevention: Money belt or front pocket. Backpack in front in crowds.
  • Scams: Friendship bracelet on Las Ramblas, shell game near tourist spots
  • Cars: Never leave valuables visible in rental cars — smash-and-grab occurs
